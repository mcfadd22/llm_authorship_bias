## Incidents worth knowing before the API run

- **Runaway whitespace under the JSON grammar.** Whitespace is always a legal next token, so a
  model can emit it forever; with no `max_tokens` such calls ran to the provider's context
  limit and the trickle kept read timeouts from firing. Fixed: `max_tokens=4096` on
  OpenAI-compatible calls, a 240 s wall-clock deadline per attempt, and a letters-only
  degeneracy check. Six rows hit the cap and were retried successfully.
- **Provider routing.** OpenRouter spread `gpt-oss-120b` across ~13 providers; several stall
  or return truncated JSON. Pinning to one provider turned its overload 429s into instant
  failures. What worked: `provider.order` Groq-first with fallbacks and an ignore list; 81%
  of gpt-oss rows came from Groq at ~1 s per call. `qwen/qwen3-32b` has only two providers
  with structured output; DeepInfra served 99%.
- **Concurrency.** 192 concurrent calls collapsed; 48 per judge was stable. Connection reuse
  was disabled (fresh connection per call) after stale pooled sockets hung workers.
- **Laptop sleep** froze the run once; workers now run under `caffeinate`.
- `anthropic` is imported at module top in `orchestrate.py`, so it must be installed even for
  OpenAI-compatible judges.

## Results (pilot; design.md §6.5 families; WCB B=4999, Holm per family)

Survives Holm at .05: 5 of 30 contrasts. Effects are small (scale 1–7).

| hypothesis | judge | contrast | est | p_holm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 blame | qwen3-32b | generic_ai vs none | −0.66 | 0.0008 |
| H3 ability↔diligence | gpt-oss-120b | generic_ai vs none | −0.28 | 0.0008 |
| H3 ability↔diligence | qwen3-32b | self vs none | −0.26 | 0.023 |
| H3 ability↔diligence | qwen3-32b | generic_ai vs none | −0.52 | 0.0008 |
| H3 ability↔diligence | qwen3-32b | other_model_pooled vs none | −0.31 | 0.0018 |

H4 (pooled rival − self), the direct test of the core question: null on all three outcomes
for both judges (|est| ≤ 0.12, p ≥ 0.10). The label effects that do appear are "an AI system"
vs no label, shifting the explanation toward *ability gap* and (Qwen) lowering blame — the
opposite direction from wave 1's Claude judges, which shifted AI labels toward *diligence
lapse*.

**Clean-code control.** Detection rate on buggy items and false-positive rate on clean twins
barely move with the label:

| judge | detection (buggy), min–max across labels | false positive (clean), min–max |
|---|---|---|
| qwen3-32b | 0.88–0.91 | 0.27–0.35 |
| gpt-oss-120b | 0.83–0.87 | 0.23–0.25 |

So the label is not changing whether these judges see a bug; it changes how they explain it.
False-positive rates of a quarter to a third on clean twins are high enough that the
`twin_not_minimal` flags and the twins themselves deserve a look before wave 2 proper.

**Authorship check** (crude keyword coding, buggy items): under `self`, 64% of Qwen answers
name Qwen and 78% of gpt-oss answers name GPT; under `none`, gpt-oss says "AI" 32% and never
names GPT; under `generic_ai`, gpt-oss names GPT 48% of the time. The label takes.
